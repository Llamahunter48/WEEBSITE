Hiking in New Zealand: Understanding the Environment

New Zealand’s tracks are amazing, but our environment changes fast, so it’s important to be prepared. From muddy bush to exposed alpine ridges, each landscape needs slightly different gear and planning.

Native Bush Tracks

A lot of NZ walks go through dense native bush with tree roots, mud, and limited views.


What it’s like:

Shady, often damp, uneven ground, roots and rocks, streams, sometimes slippery boardwalks.


Tips for this environment:

Wear grippy shoes or boots, watch your footing on roots, carry a light rain jacket (bush can stay wet even on sunny days), and stay on the marked track because it’s easy to lose your way in thick forest.

Coastal and Beach Walks

We have long beaches and coastal headlands with big views but also strong sun and wind.


What it’s like:

Soft sand, exposed cliffs, strong UV, sea breeze, and sometimes tides blocking parts of the beach.


Tips for this environment:

Use strong sun protection (hat, sunscreen, sunglasses), bring extra water, check tide times before you go, and be careful near cliff edges in wind or rain.
